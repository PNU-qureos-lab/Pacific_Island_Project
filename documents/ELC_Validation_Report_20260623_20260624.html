2. What we actually did

The numbered folders document a six-step empirical line calibration. Each step below names its input, operation, and written evidence.

1

Inventory source images

Identify tarp captures, camera module, band, filename, and source path.

Evidence: 01_inventory/*.csv
Expected: 16 tarp captures × 10 bands.
2

Extract tarp ROIs

Measure a 10×10 pixel ROI on the 3%, 36%, and 56% tarps; calculate DN mean, CV, and saturation.

Evidence: tarp_roi_qc.csv + ROI JSON
Rows: 480 (24 Jun), 477 (23 Jun).
3

Convert DN to radiance

Use exposure, ISO, black level, and the MicaSense radiometric metadata to calculate radiance for each tarp ROI.

Evidence: 03_tarp_radiance/*.csv
Output: L3, L36, L56 by capture and band.
4

Screen calibration pairs

Use 3% and 36% as training targets. Reject pairs when the 36% target fails saturation QC. Reserve 56% for validation.

Evidence: valid/rejected pair CSVs
Retained: 159 (24 Jun), 56 (23 Jun).
5

Estimate ELC coefficients

Fit slope and intercept for every valid capture and band, then use the median capture coefficient as the final daily value.

Evidence: per-capture and final coefficient CSVs
Model: ρ = aL + b.
6

Apply ELC to all images

Convert each radiance pixel to reflectance using its band coefficient; record min, max, mean, below-0, and above-1 statistics.

Evidence: 06_ELC_reflectance
Output: 51,720 non-empty TIFFs total.

Training targetsNominal ρ=0.03 and ρ=0.36. Two targets define each capture-level empirical line.
Validation targetNominal ρ=0.56, used only when unsaturated. It does not alter the fit.
Final daily coefficientMedian of the valid capture-level slopes and intercepts for each band.
Acceptance logic in this reviewPrefer ≥8 captures per band; <5 is weak. Slope CV <5% is stable when the sample size is adequate.
24 June: 15–16 capture-level fits support every band. This is enough to interpret the low coefficient CV as real stability.
23 June: all 103 rejected pairs carry the reason 36_PERCENT_SATURATED. Band 9 has one fit, so its 0% CV is mathematically uninformative.
